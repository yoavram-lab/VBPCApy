# Modern VBPCA defaults study

## Status

This document preregisters the settings screen requested in issues
[#214](https://github.com/yoavram-lab/VBPCApy/issues/214) and
[#225](https://github.com/yoavram-lab/VBPCApy/issues/225). The registered
screen configuration and seed schedule are generated by
`_modern_defaults_design.py`. Do not change the design after inspecting screen
outcomes. Corrections require a new design version and independent seeds.

## Questions

We will test whether the current shape-routed `recommend_config()` settings
remain appropriate after the dense backend changes, and whether modern
post-factor mean/RMS ordering changes fitted or selected models relative to the
MATLAB update order.

The study addresses four practical questions:

1. Do prior, probe-fraction, and convergence settings transfer across matrix
   shapes and missingness mechanisms?
2. Can another setting reduce selected-rank error or iteration count without
   harming held-out prediction or uncertainty calibration?
3. Does `bias_update_order="post_factor"` remain non-inferior to `"legacy"`
   when both use `compat_mode="modern"`?
4. Are any conclusions retained under heavy-tailed noise and at the
   5,846-feature by 2,504-sample genomics scale?

## Stages

### Screen

The screen crosses 30 candidates with 14 simulation regimes and three paired
replicates. Six anchors include the current recommendation under both bias
orders, three fixed probe fractions, and the estimator defaults. Twenty-four
scrambled Sobol candidates vary the following fields around each regime's
current recommendation:

- the three prior hyperparameters and initial prior variance;
- internal probe fraction;
- broad-prior iterations and maximum-iteration scale;
- convergence patience, RMS window and tolerances, subspace-angle tolerance,
  and relative cost tolerance;
- production, no-RMS, and angle-plus-cost criterion policies;
- legacy and post-factor bias update ordering.

The design contains tall, square, wide, large, and scaled genomics shapes.
Complete, MCAR, MAR, lower-tail MNAR, and block missingness are represented.
Two regimes use Student-t noise with three degrees of freedom, scaled to the
same variance as the Gaussian condition. Two null-rank regimes assess
false-positive component selection.

### Independent confirmation

At most four screen candidates advance alongside the mandatory current
post-factor and legacy anchors. Confirmation uses nine new regimes, 12 new
replicates, and a disjoint seed range. Candidate identities are written to a
new immutable manifest after applying the screen rules. They are not chosen by
manual inspection.

### Genomics-scale confirmation

The same mandatory anchors and promoted candidates are evaluated at 5,846
features by 2,504 samples under complete, MAR, and MNAR observation patterns.
This stage uses three new replicates and another disjoint seed range. It begins
only after the moderate-size confirmation passes, because each selected-rank
sweep is expensive.

## Data generation and missingness

Matrices follow the package convention of features by samples. Gaussian
conditions use a linear low-rank signal plus independent Gaussian noise.
Student-t conditions replace the noise with a variance-matched t distribution
with three degrees of freedom.

The missing fraction is 15% before the external evaluation holdout:

- MCAR uses independent Bernoulli masking.
- MAR uses a fully observed anchor feature as the predictor in a logistic
  missingness model for the remaining features. The intercept is calibrated to
  the target missing fraction, so missingness depends only on observed data.
- MNAR censors the lower tail of each feature using its own values.
- Block missingness removes one contiguous feature-by-sample block.

Every training mask retains support in each nonempty row and column. A separate
10% support-preserving holdout is used only for evaluation. Candidate rank is
selected using an additional internal probe set and predictive RMS. Candidate
sets include rank zero through five components above the generating rank.

## Outcomes

The primary statistical outcomes are:

- absolute selected-rank error and exact-rank recovery;
- false-positive selection probability in rank-zero regimes;
- held-out RMSE;
- 95% predictive interval coverage and interval score;
- subspace error relative to the generating loading space;
- selected-fit and candidate-level iteration-budget hit rates;
- total iterations and convergence reason;
- RMS two-cycle amplitude and the fraction of RMS increases.

Wall time and peak resident memory are resource diagnostics. They receive zero
weight in candidate promotion.

## Paired inference and promotion

Every candidate uses the same generated data, observation mask, external
holdout, and initialization seed as its anchor within a regime and replicate.
Uncertainty uses a paired, regime-stratified bootstrap with 5,000 resamples and
a frozen seed.

A screen candidate is eligible only when the 95% confidence bound satisfies
all registered non-inferiority limits relative to the current post-factor
recommendation:

- relative held-out RMSE increase no greater than 1%;
- relative interval-score increase no greater than 2%;
- coverage decrease no greater than 0.02;
- rank-MAE increase no greater than 0.10;
- null-selection-rate increase no greater than 0.05.

Eligible candidates are Pareto filtered on rank MAE, held-out RMSE, and total
iterations. If more than four remain, ordering is by paired rank-MAE
difference, held-out RMSE difference, iteration ratio, and candidate ID.

Confirmation applies the same safety limits. A settings change additionally
requires either a rank-MAE reduction of at least 0.10 or a total-iteration
reduction of at least 20%. Results are reported overall and separately by
shape, missingness mechanism, and noise model. No default changes are made
from screen results alone.
